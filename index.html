Clinical decision timing

How clinicians time escalation and referral

You will read 21 anonymised patient records, each unfolding across several contacts, and say what you would do at each point. There are no right answers supplied and nothing is being tested — your judgement is the reference.

  • 1One contact at a time. You see the record exactly as it stood at that moment. Decide what you would do next, then lock it in. You cannot revise a locked answer — later contacts reveal information you did not have, and an answer changed in hindsight is no longer the judgement we need.
  • 2Then the whole story. Once you have answered every contact in a case, all of it is shown together and you are asked when escalation and referral should have happened — as a window, not a single point: the earliest reasonable moment and the last acceptable one.
  • 3Work in sittings. Progress is kept in this browser. Close the tab and return to the same link on the same machine to carry on. Use Save my answers at any time to download what you have so far.

Records are drawn from published, de-identified case reports. Identifiers, diagnoses and outcomes have been removed, and cases are labelled T01–T21 in an order that carries no meaning. Please do not search for the source — a record whose ending you know can no longer be judged as it stood.
